MediPass

From a safe check today to medicine made for one person tomorrow.

The idea

One personal health record, kept by the patient, that an AI agent can use to pick and buy the right medicine at the right moment. It reads your medicines, allergies, conditions, gene results and live data from a ring or band. A doctor stays in the loop for prescriptions. Payments between agent, doctor and pharmacy happen automatically on Solana.

Why it is different

Other apps tell you what a medicine does. MediPass tells you what it does in you.

Personal, not general
A usual interaction checker compares one medicine with another. MediPass also uses gene results, so it can tell whether your body clears a drug fast, slowly or not at all. To our knowledge few consumer apps do this today.
At the moment of the decision
The warning appears when you buy or order, including over-the-counter medicines such as ibuprofen, which a prescription record does not contain.
One place, not four
Medicines, allergies, kidney values, gene results and your own notes sit together instead of in a doctor's system, a lab report, a drawer and your memory.
Only what is needed is shared
A pharmacist gets a short lived code with the warnings only, never the whole record.

Where the data comes from, and its limits

Sources
The patient record (medicines from e-prescriptions, letters, diagnoses, lab values such as kidney function), a one off gene test that covers around ten to fifteen genes, and the person's own notes on over-the-counter medicines, effects and side effects.
Limits we respect, to our current understanding
Health and gene data have special protection under the GDPR and need explicit consent, so the data should stay on the person's own device. In Germany, genetic tests may only be ordered by doctors, so MediPass takes in the result and never orders a test. Third party apps cannot simply read the national patient record, as access runs through the insurer's app. For the start, people enter their medicines by hand or scan their medication plan. These legal points were not freshly verified, so check them before relying on them.

Two kinds of medicine, two kinds of personal

Small molecules

Aspirin, ibuprofen, clopidogrel

You cannot redesign a small molecule for one person, because a changed molecule no longer fits the enzyme and stops working. What you can personalise is the choice and the dose. Gene results such as CYP2C19 or DPYD predict who clears a drug fast or slowly. This is where MediPass works today.

Antibodies and mRNA therapies

Made for one patient

A tumour is different in every person. Its mutations can be read, and a therapy can be built against them. Moderna and Merck report that their individualised neoantigen therapy intismeran autogene met its main goal in a Phase 3 melanoma trial in August 2026. This is the long term bridge, and it is not cheap or fast yet.

"Personalised medicine" means different things here. The method depends on the drug type, so we keep the two paths apart.

Four year plan

2026 to 2027

Check and order

Pilot with a few pharmacies and doctors. Over-the-counter orders and doctor approved prescriptions. Rules reviewed by pharmacists. Regulatory path clarified for medical software.

2027 to 2028

Patient owned data

Connect rings and bands, labs and gene reports with consent. Pay per use between agents and services instead of selling the data.

2028 to 2029

Closed loop

Track whether a medicine worked and whether side effects appeared. Use it to improve advice and doses for the person.

2029 to 2030

Bridge to made to order

Partner with makers of personalised therapies. MediPass becomes the consented data and ordering layer between patient, doctor and manufacturer.

This is a plan, not a forecast. It depends on regulation, partners and funding.

Policy proposal, a consented health data pass

The problem
Health data is scattered and incomplete. Companies that collect it often sell it on. At the same time people take medicines that interact with their own genes and other drugs, and AI agents are starting to buy things for us.
The proposal
A patient owned health record with a duty on every AI agent to check it before buying a medicine. Prescriptions need a human clinician in the loop. Every use leaves an audit trail, and the data is shared only for a stated purpose and for a limited time, as the pharmacy code already does.
Who is responsible
The clinician who approves a prescription. The operator of the agent for its safety checks and its logs. The pharmacy for dispensing. The patient for the accuracy of what they enter. Regulators certify the rules, as for any medical software.
Trade offs
Faster ordering must not mean lower quality of care, so red flags stop the order. Rules can be wrong or out of date, so every verdict names its source and says it does not replace a pharmacist. Rich health data can be misused, so we minimise what is shared. Cheap and fast access can push people to skip a conversation with a professional. Gene data can reveal family members, and access can be unequal.

MediPass does not replace advice from a doctor or pharmacist.
